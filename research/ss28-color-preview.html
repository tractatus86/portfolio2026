1. What the report claims (its own words, source text.md, article of 22 Oct 2026)

  • Theme: "MASTERWORKS"; "Shades are moving beyond traditional classifications, while chromatic boundaries are becoming more fluid".
  • First palette line: "traces atmospheric flows, driven by a desire for lightness and breathing space"; "blues, greens and purples"; "particular expression in the blue and purple families", "airy, diffused shades are interacting with more assertive pigment intensities".
  • Blues: "becoming bolder, more pigmented and less muted"; "Royal blue is making a comeback, slightly desaturated yet vivid and luminous"; "sky blue is gaining depth"; "dark blues receding in favor of brighter, airier and more digital-inspired tones".
  • 03 Sonar Blue (new): "turquoise blue", "softened and slightly desaturated", "oscillates between blue and green"; "gaining ground in menswear, particularly in summer pieces"; growing "from Brazil to Europe, the United States and especially China".
  • Purples: "A luminous, grayish mauve is returning"; "More saturated mauve tones are giving way to a magnetic violet that is vivid, dense and impactful"; "A whitened, muted violet, carried over from Autumn-Winter 2027-28".
  • 05 Magnetic Violet (new): "a dense, bluish violet"; Heuritech: "strong regional potential", "still relatively understated in womenswear globally", "significant traction in certain markets, particularly India", momentum "extending to the Middle East", also "in menswear".

2. What the data shows (SS26 to SS28)

Method (computed, not printed): Lab values computed by Agent 2 from the measured hex values in data.csv, sRGB to CIE Lab, D65, with C* = sqrt(a^2 + b^2) as chroma and h = hue angle in degrees (about 180 = green, 270 = blue, 300-330 = violet, 0 = red-pink). Python in ~/.hermes/venvs/moodboard. Scratch scripts are not filed; numbers can be recomputed from data.csv.

Data limits first: the report gives earlier-season swatches (SS26, SS27, AW 27-28) unnamed and only for blues and purples. There is no earlier-season data for greens, so no green shift can be stated. SS28 line: 7 colors (3 blues, 3 greens, 1 violet). The purples evolution shows 3 SS28 swatches, of which only Magnetic Violet is in the 7-color line; Mauve Stoneware and Violine are not in the line list.

4. Report's claims against the data

Supported by the data: - "Dark blues receding": SS28 has none below L* 38.6, earlier seasons 1 to 2 each (2b). - "Brighter blues, more pigmented and less muted": SS28 mean C* 28.1 versus 19.3 (SS27) and 11.0 (AW). Holds against the last two seasons; SS26 was 33.6, so versus SS26 it is not more pigmented. - "Royal blue slightly desaturated": Ultra Blue C* 37.2 is below SS26 #4C7AD2 (51.5) and SS27 #1F4B90 (43.9). But the report never says which earlier swatch is "royal blue"; the match is ours. "Luminous": L* 38.6 is lighter than SS27 #1F4B90 (32.6) and darker than SS26 (52.0). - Sonar "oscillates between blue and green": h 211 sits between Sky Green (191) and Halo Blue (253); this fits. "Softened, slightly desaturated": C* 31.4, below 34.9 (SS26 #0084B1) but no matching previous swatch to compare, so only partly testable. - "Luminous, grayish mauve is returning": Mauve Stoneware (L* 67.1, C* 5.6) is near-identical (dE 2.4) to an SS26 swatch, so "returning" fits. By hue it is a pinkish neutral (h 3), more gray than mauve. - "More saturated mauve tones giving way to a magnetic violet that is vivid, dense": AW mauves are C* 14.9 to 21.8; Magnetic Violet is 35.4 and darker than AW #B99AB9. Fits. - Sonar growth "from Brazil to Europe, the United States and especially China": those four are positive in the read-offs (2.8, 3.8, 2.2, 5.2), China highest. - Magnetic Violet "particularly India" (womenswear): India is highest growth (4.4) and highest share (0.13) in womenswear. "Middle East": menswear 7.0, highest menswear value. Not supported, partly supported or untestable: - "Sky blue is gaining depth": the lightest blue falls from L* 77.3 (SS27) and 74.7 (AW) to 69.0 (Halo Blue), and Halo is similar in chroma, so lower lightness supports "depth"; but the report does not say which swatch is sky blue. - "A whitened, muted violet carried over from AW 2027-28": Violine (L* 38.6) is not whitened, it is dark and muted. A lighter candidate is Mauve Stoneware, but it is closer to an SS26 swatch than to an AW one. The report does not tie the phrase to a numbered swatch; not testable. - "Magnetic Violet ... still relatively understated in womenswear globally": womenswear Worldwide share reads about 0.05 against India 0.13; low relative to India, but there is no benchmark against other colors. Untestable. - "Strong regional potential": regional spread is wide (menswear -10.9 to 7.0; womenswear -9.2 to 4.4), consistent with regional contrast. It does not show the same markets strong in both genders (India reverses). Report text says "also in menswear" without India's negative menswear read-off. - "Sonar gaining ground in menswear": the printed Worldwide growth is 1,33 % (small), with 4 of 10 regions negative. - "Dark blues receding in favor of ... digital-inspired tones": "digital" is not measurable from the data. - "Chromatic boundaries becoming more fluid": the hue sequence (2e) has no break larger than 49 degrees and Sonar fills the 191-253 gap; this is consistent but is not a test, because earlier-season hue spacing for greens is not in the data.

5. Limits

  • Bubble values are read off by pixel measurement: growth about +/-0.3 points, share about +/-0.002. Not printed numbers.
  • p4 (Sonar menswear) x-axis tick labels (0,06 0,08 0,09 0,11 0,13) are unevenly spaced numbers on evenly spaced gridlines; a linear axis was assumed, so Sonar share values are uncertain and may be off more than the stated tolerance. Do not draw conclusions from Sonar share differences of a few hundredths.
  • Y tick labels on p4 and on the womenswear chart share the same rounding pattern (even gridlines, rounded labels).
  • Overlapping bubbles (Sonar menswear: Brazil/Middle East and United States/Worldwide; violet menswear: United States/Worldwide; violet womenswear: United States/Worldwide and China/Europe) have estimated centers.
  • Worldwide is an outline ring. Check: Sonar menswear ring read-off 1.2 against printed 1,33 matches. Magnetic Violet womenswear ring read-off 0.6 against printed 1,7 does not match (1.1 points). Worldwide positions are the least reliable, and violet menswear Worldwide (-2.0) cannot be checked because no menswear headline is printed.
  • Axes differ between charts (menswear violet share 0.03-0.07; womenswear 0-0.14; Sonar 0.06-0.13). "Market share (%)" values written as 0.09 etc. leave the unit unclear (fraction or percent); treat as relative position only.
  • Growth is described as "SS28 vs SS27" in the printed headline; whether the axis growth is percent or percentage points is not stated.
  • Earlier-season swatches are unnamed, so a swatch in SS26 and one in SS28 can be compared only by measured color, not by name; "same color returning" is our reading by dE, not the report's statement. The slide gives only vertical position, not links, for purples.
  • Hex are measured from slide swatches (sRGB, no color profile known); screen and print rendering can differ, and PNG swatches are not a lab-measured standard. Lab values are computed with D65 sRGB assumption.
  • Only blues and purples have evolution data; greens are not covered. No source, sample size or period is printed for Heuritech.
  • Sample sizes per family-season are 1 to 5 swatches; means are descriptive.

6. Our interpretation (Agent 2; not a trend verdict)

  • Reading of the shift: blues reset from low-chroma, dark and gray-blue neutrals (AW) to clearer mid-tone blues with a greener cyan edge (Sonar) and a mid-dark cobalt (Ultra); the dark end is ceded and the light end becomes a gray-blue (Halo). In practice the SS28 blue story is mid-value, clear, and cooler-greener, not dark-and-moody and not pastel.
  • For a CMF designer: if we propose blue textile colors, the data suggests clear mid-value blues in L* 40-70 and C* 15-40 are in line with this report; a navy below L* 30 or a very low-chroma gray-blue would sit outside what the report shows for SS28. This is not a market claim; the report says nothing about navy as a staple.
  • Sonar Blue: a teal-leaning blue (h 211) can bridge blue and green in one palette (it sits between Sky Green and Halo Blue). It is the one SS28 color with no close earlier match. For textile, a piece-dyed or yarn-dyed teal blue might be a bridging color between a green and a blue ground; whether it dyes accurately on a given fiber is for Engineering/Lab.
  • Magnetic Violet is measurably close to an SS26 swatch (dE 5.8), so "new" is partly naming. Violine and Mauve Stoneware are close to earlier swatches (dE 4.0 and 2.4): the purples line looks more like a reordering and a re-chroma-ing of known purples than a new region of color space. Hypothesis, not tested.
  • Gender: the data shows the same violet reading differently by gender (India), which for design means the violet may need a market-specific story, not one global one. Sonar Blue is menswear-led in the report; whether it would travel to womenswear is unknown from this data.
  • Fluidity: the hue sequence is nearly continuous from green to violet; one could design combinations by adjacent hues (green to teal to blue). That is a styling idea, not something the data proves.
  • Fiber/finish: the report's "airy, diffused, filtered" language points to sheer or low-contrast surfaces (gauze, light-weight woven, tonal heathers). Construction first; heather later. Feasibility is Engineering's.
