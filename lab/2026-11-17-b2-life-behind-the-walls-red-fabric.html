Silhouettes on sheer red cloth: B2 Architecture, "Life Behind the Walls" (architecture / exhibition design)

"Translucent red walls that show people only as shadows."

Theme: T4 Institutional critique and the apparatus · Topic: Textile structure · Status: idea

Proposal

Theme reason: cloth and timber are used as the apparatus that stages what a visitor can see, hide and cross.

One-line idea: "Translucent red walls that show people only as shadows."

Why it matters: Fact: B2 Architecture (Prague) built a 1,500 m2 exhibition on life in North Korea, based on a children's book by Nina Špitálníková, from red-painted timber frames hung with red and pink translucent fabric; a darker second gallery uses tall translucent panels printed with human silhouettes that overlap portraits and testimonies of defectors. The structure is lightweight modular timber and stretched textile; the timber went to another project and the textiles and flooring to several theatres (Dezeen, 16 Nov 2026). Interpretation: sheer printed cloth works here as a spatial layer: it lets you glimpse, not read, which is what the exhibition is about. For an interiors and soft-materials designer it is a clear case of fabric doing architectural work, with reuse planned from the start. Subject is sensitive; keep the post about the material and spatial method, not the politics (to confirm tone with Hermes).

Format and visual direction: Carousel, 3 to 4 slides. (1) Red timber frames and fabric hangings in the bright gallery. (2) Printed silhouette panels in the dark gallery. (3) A sketch of the sheer as a layer: front, print, glimpse. (4) A line on reuse of the textiles. Palette: saturated red and pink against dark. Owner could render: a sheer panel with a printed silhouette, backlit, in Vizcom or a quick render.

Alt text: Tall translucent red fabric panels printed with dark human silhouettes hang in a dim gallery, with a statue glowing behind them.

Opening line: A sheer wall can hide people and show them at the same time.

Idea: The design choice to admire is the fabric: a printed sheer lets silhouettes read from far away and dissolve up close. Think of it as a density study at room scale. The reuse of the textiles by theatres is a good, concrete note on end of life for event fabrics.

Source line: https://www.dezeen.com/2026/11/16/life-behind-the-walls-north-korea-exhibition-b2-architecture-prague/ (Dezeen, 16 Nov 2026)
