The New Raw "Buoy": harbour rope to rocking seat

"Rope in, seat out": discarded Rotterdam harbour rope, shredded, pelletised and 3D-printed into stools whose color comes from the source rope.

T9 Wear, fragility and the trace of time · Sustainability

Why it matters

Lineage: rope and net as the original textile structure. Link: a worn fiber becomes a surface with no added pigment, relevant to recycled-content color (CMF) and to polypropylene-based soft materials.

The idea

"No pigment added. The color is whatever the rope was."

Per Dezeen (1 Nov 2026), founder Foteini Setaki collected plastic ropes, recycled them to pellets in Maastricht and printed Buoy stools in Rotterdam. They are shortlisted for a 2026 Dezeen Award (sustainable design). Pointed bases make the seat rock like a buoy. letting feedstock set the color is a CMF decision, not only a sustainability claim. the same approach could inform undyed recycled-fiber palettes.
