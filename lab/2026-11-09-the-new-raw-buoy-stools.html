The New Raw "Buoy": harbour rope to rocking seat

"Rope in, seat out": discarded Rotterdam harbour rope, shredded, pelletised and 3D-printed into stools whose color comes from the source rope.

Theme: T9 Wear, fragility and the trace of time · Topic: Sustainability · Status: idea

Proposal

Theme reason: worn harbour rope keeps its history in the color of the new object.

One-line idea: "Rope in, seat out": discarded Rotterdam harbour rope, shredded, pelletised and 3D-printed into stools whose color comes from the source rope.

Why it matters: Lineage: rope and net as the original textile structure. Link: a worn fiber becomes a surface with no added pigment, relevant to recycled-content color (CMF) and to polypropylene-based soft materials.

Format and visual direction: Carousel of 3 to 4: flow diagram (rope, pellets, print, stool) drawn as your own clean illustration; 3D render of a layered-print surface in muted rope colors (rust, gray-blue, faded orange); a swatch strip showing hue variation that comes from the feedstock.

Alt text: Diagram showing rope turning into pellets and then into a layered 3D-printed stool.

Opening line: "No pigment added. The color is whatever the rope was."

Idea: Per Dezeen (1 Nov 2026), founder Foteini Setaki collected plastic ropes, recycled them to pellets in Maastricht and printed Buoy stools in Rotterdam. They are shortlisted for a 2026 Dezeen Award (sustainable design). Pointed bases make the seat rock like a buoy. Interpretation: letting feedstock set the color is a CMF decision, not only a sustainability claim. Hypothesis: the same approach could inform undyed recycled-fiber palettes. To confirm: recyclability of the additive package beyond what Setaki states.

Source line: https://www.dezeen.com/2026/11/01/buoy-stools-the-new-raw/
