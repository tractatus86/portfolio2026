Hard Art: abstraction that hangs, drapes and stretches (fine art)

"Abstraction you can feel" - MoMA PS1's Hard Art, read through soft and flexible materials.

Theme: T1 Fabric as sculpture, sculpture as cloth · Topic: Textile structure · Status: idea

Proposal

Theme reason: unstretched, draped cloth and canvas as sculpture.

One-line idea: "Abstraction you can feel" - MoMA PS1's Hard Art, read through soft and flexible materials.

Why it matters: The show (opened 5 Nov 2026) covers abstraction by Black artists in the US from 1969 to today, 40+ artists, 90+ works. The artist list includes Sam Gilliam, Senga Nengudi, Barbara Chase-Riboud, Howardena Pindell, Maren Hassinger. Interpretation: several of these artists are known for unstretched, draped or tied materials (Gilliam's draped canvases, Nengudi's stretched nylon); which works are on view is to confirm. Link to textiles: surface, tension and drape as design variables.

Format and visual direction: Carousel of 3. Slide 1: own photo of a draped or tensioned fabric against a plain wall. Slide 2: detail of weave under raking light. Slide 3: a quiet text card with the exhibition name and dates. Color cues: the stained, saturated color fields of the era (to confirm from press images); material cues: raw cotton, nylon, dye bleed.

Alt text: A length of dyed fabric hangs from two points in a gallery-white room, folding into soft diagonal drapes.

Opening line: What happens to a painting when you let go of the stretcher?

Idea: MoMA PS1 opened Hard Art this week, tracing abstraction by Black artists from 1969 on. For anyone who designs with cloth, the period's habit of letting material hang, sag and stretch is a free lesson in drape and tension. The pairing shows fabric behaving as the structure itself, not a surface applied to one.

Source line: https://press.moma.org/exhibition/hard-art/ (MoMA press, 4 Nov 2026)
