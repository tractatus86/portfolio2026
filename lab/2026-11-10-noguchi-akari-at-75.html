Noguchi Akari at 75: paper, bamboo and the idea of light

"Weightless by construction": Akari are collapsible washi-and-bamboo lights; the structure is the form.

T8 Asia and postcolonial frame · Material innovation

Why it matters

Lineage: Gifu chochin lantern craft, 1951, then 200+ models until 1988. Link: a translucent fiber sheet over a rib structure is a textile problem (tension, grain, light transmission), directly relevant to backlit fabric panels in interiors.

The idea

"Noguchi did not draw a lamp. He drew how paper holds a shape."

The Noguchi Museum's "No Ordinary Light: Akari at 75" opened 4 Nov 2026 and runs to 5 Sep 2027. Per the museum, Noguchi began Akari in Gifu in 1951, kept the handmade collapsible structure, and added new shapes, electric bulbs and wire bases; they are still made by Ozeki & Co. from mulberry-bark washi and bamboo. The show also revisits the 1951 debut at the Eames House. a structure that folds flat and still reads as sculpture is a good model for soft goods that ship compact. the same rib-and-skin logic could be tested with a translucent knit over a light frame.
