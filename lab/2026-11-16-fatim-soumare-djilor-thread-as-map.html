Fatim Soumaré, "Djilor": a spool of thread as a map of place

Seven monumental cotton works whose threads run like vines and carry objects gathered from Cape Verde to the Maghreb and Brazil.

Theme: T6 Place and nature · Topic: Textile structure · Status: idea

Proposal

Theme reason: a village founded by unwinding a thread, and monumental cotton works that turn a journey into a map.

One-line idea: Seven monumental cotton works whose threads run like vines and carry objects gathered from Cape Verde to the Maghreb and Brazil.

Why it matters: Fact: Selebe Yoon, Dakar, shows "Djilor", Fatim Soumaré's first solo show at the gallery, 6 Nov 2026 to 27 Mar 2027, opening 18 Nov 2026, alongside the 16th Dakar Biennale. Seven new works combine cotton with terracotta beads, shells, spinning wheels, anchors, old manillas, sand, fish scales and wool offcuts from Amazigh carpets (gallery page). The village's origin story, per the page, is a spool of hand-spun cotton unwound to find the place. Soumaré learned textile craft from her mother, a traditional dyer. Interpretation: structure first: the thread is the plan, and knots and loops decide where weight and objects sit. For an interiors and soft-materials designer it is a lesson in open cotton constructions that hold mass.

Format and visual direction: Carousel, 4 slides. (1) Hanging cotton work in the studio. (2) Knotted hangings outdoors. (3) Detail: dense tufted cotton against open thread. (4) One line on thread as a map. Palette: raw cotton, teal wall, brick and ochre from the carpet. You could make a small knotted or netted cotton study and photograph it against a plain wall.

Alt text: A large cream cotton thread work hangs from the ceiling of a teal room, with dense bands of cotton and dark stripes near the bottom.

Opening line: What if the first line on a map was a thread?

Idea: Soumaré starts from one spool and builds a landscape. Compare open net with dense tuft in the same cotton: that contrast is the construction lesson. Set her hanging beside your own open-structure sample and ask how few yarns still carry a place.

Source line: https://www.selebe-yoon.com/exhibitions1/fatim-soumare-djilor (Selebe Yoon gallery page, show from 6 Nov 2026; page opened and read; exact page publication date not shown)
