JR and Giovanni Bonotto: Veronese's "Wedding at Cana" woven as a tapestry (fine art / art history)

"A 1563 painting, turned into a photo, turned into cloth."

Theme: T3 Dematerialization vs rematerialization · Topic: Material innovation · Status: idea

Proposal

Theme reason: a huge digital image becomes a physical weave, and the loom has to be argued with to make it.

One-line idea: "A 1563 painting, turned into a photo, turned into cloth."

Why it matters: Fact: at the 61st Venice Biennale, JR's project Il Gesto reinterprets Paolo Veronese's 1563 painting The Wedding at Cana as a tapestry woven with Fondazione Bonotto, 4.30 m by 7.80 m, on view until 24 Nov 2026. It took 600 hours of research and weaving. Blue yarns and black shadow tones come from recycled plastic from household chemical and soap containers, combined with virgin wool, organic cotton and Japanese washi paper. Bonotto says the file was too large for the mechanical looms, which kept crashing (designboom, 9 May 2026). Lineage: Veronese's painting hung at San Giorgio Maggiore for over two centuries before moving to the Louvre (same page); tapestry as the old medium for carrying painting into rooms. Interpretation: the story is about construction limits: yarn count, loom file size and yarn choice decide how much of a photograph survives in cloth. Hypothesis: the recycled-plastic yarns in shadow areas are a case for a Material innovation post; yarn spec and performance are not stated, to confirm.

Format and visual direction: Carousel, 3 slides. (1) The tapestry in its room, (2) a crop of the weave at detail scale, (3) one line on the loom problem. Palette: black, white, sky blue. Grayscale with one blue. You could make a 2D study turning one of your own photographs into a limited-yarn weave plan and show what is lost.

Alt text: A huge black, white and blue woven tapestry of a banquet scene with columns and a giant hand in the sky hangs on the wall of a Venetian room.

Opening line: A painting from 1563 is now a tapestry that crashed the looms.

Idea: JR starts from a photograph and Bonotto has to decide, yarn by yarn, what the loom can hold. For a textile designer the interesting part is the translation: recycled-plastic blues, washi and wool each give a different surface. Pick one image of your own and ask how many yarns it really needs.

Source line: https://www.designboom.com/art/a-little-theater-of-life-jr-weaves-monumental-tapestry-of-community-care-in-venice/ (designboom, 9 May 2026; on view until 24 Nov 2026). Older than the 48 h window; I used it because the show closes soon.
