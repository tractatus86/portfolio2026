Aurèlia Muñoz: tapestry that leaves the wall

"Beings in the air": macramé and tapestry as free-hanging structure. Muñoz's knotted forms drop the wall and become vertical, suspended bodies.

Theme: T1 Fabric as sculpture, sculpture as cloth · Topic: Material innovation · Status: idea

Proposal

Theme reason: tapestry and macrame leaving the wall to become sculpture.

One-line idea: "Beings in the air": macramé and tapestry as free-hanging structure. Muñoz's knotted forms drop the wall and become vertical, suspended bodies.

Why it matters: Lineage: nouvelle tapisserie and the Catalan tapestry school, Lausanne Biennials 1965 to 1983 (MACBA). Link to textiles/interiors: knot, tension and verticality are the same logic as headliner, pleat and suspended acoustic soft goods.

Format and visual direction: Carousel of 4. (1) Own pencil study of a knot-hang structure; (2) close crop of own macramé or cord test; (3) side-by-side of your drawing and a reference detail described in the caption; (4) a short note on hessian, sail canvas, handmade paper. Palette: undyed jute, rope beige, one blue-gray. Plain light background.

Alt text: Pencil drawing of a hanging knotted cord form beside a photographed sample of undyed knotted rope.

Opening line: "She took tapestry off the wall and let it hang in the air."

Idea: MACBA's retrospective (opened 5 Nov 2026, runs to 2 May 2027) traces fifty years from hessian paintings to macramé "birds-kites" and handmade-paper mobiles. Interpretation: the interesting move is structural. Knotting makes the surface and the support the same thing, which is a useful lens for any soft material that must carry its own load. Pairing a study of knot geometry with her verticality shows how a textile can be architecture.

Source line: https://www.macba.cat/en/exhibitions/aurelia-munoz-beings/
