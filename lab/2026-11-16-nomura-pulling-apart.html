Nomura, "Pulling Apart": an adhesive that lets go with one pull

A strong, flexible tape that holds heavy panels, then stretches and peels off cleanly when pulled, so parts can be recycled.

Theme: T9 Wear, fragility and the trace of time · Topic: Sustainability · Status: idea

Proposal

Theme reason: a bond designed for its own end, so the material leaves no trace when it comes apart.

One-line idea: A strong, flexible tape that holds heavy panels, then stretches and peels off cleanly when pulled, so parts can be recycled.

Why it matters: Fact: Tokyo studio Nomura shows the tape in "Separation: Designed to Come Apart", a Daikanyama exhibition of products made for disassembly, during Designart Tokyo (30 Oct to 8 Nov 2026). Nomura says glue on timber or metal often leaves marks or blocks recycling; here one pull undoes the bond. It uses easy-disassembly bonding technology from Aica Kogyo and is still a prototype (Dezeen, 5 Nov 2026). Interpretation: soft trim, headliners and upholstery are usually glued to panels; a peel-away bond is a design question about repair and recycling at end of life. Feasibility for textiles or automotive substrates is unknown and belongs to Engineering and Lab.

Format and visual direction: Single image or 3-slide carousel. (1) The tape pulled from a panel. (2) Your sketch of a fabric panel held by a pull tab. (3) One line: "design the exit first". Palette: pale gray-blue, white, one mid gray. You could render or photograph a fabric-on-board sample with a visible pull tab.

Alt text: A hand pulls a gray strip of tape from between a white panel and a blue plate.

Opening line: What if the way a thing comes apart were the first design decision?

Idea: Most trim is designed for how it goes on. Nomura designs the exit: one tab, one pull. Put a fabric-covered panel next to it and ask where a pull tab could sit without showing.

Source line: https://www.dezeen.com/2026/11/05/designart-tokyo-2026-highlights/ (Dezeen, 5 Nov 2026); event page https://www.designart.jp/designarttokyo2026/about
