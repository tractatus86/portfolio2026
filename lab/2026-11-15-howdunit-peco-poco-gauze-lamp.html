Howdunit, "Peco Poco": a lamp whose gauze shade unfolds like an accordion (product design)

"A lamp you open by pulling a thread-thin hook."

Theme: T10 Restoration, quiet and sensory care · Topic: Textile structure · Status: idea

Proposal

Theme reason: a slow, hand-pulled gesture and soft light, with no screen or switch; weak fit, noted in theme_note.

One-line idea: "A lamp you open by pulling a thread-thin hook."

Why it matters: Fact: at Designart Tokyo 2026 (30 Oct to 8 Nov, 10th edition), Tokyo studio Howdunit (Haruto Kamijo and Sho Tasaki) showed Peco Poco, a table lamp with a shade of delicate layers of gauzy textile. A small hook at the top lets you unfold it like an accordion and set it at different heights (Dezeen, 5 Nov 2026). The name is a Japanese onomatopoeia for a growling stomach, per Dezeen. Interpretation: the shade is a pleated, sheer structure, so height, fold depth and layer count decide how much light passes. That is a textile-structure question inside a product. Hypothesis: the same folded sheer could act as a light-diffusing trim or headliner panel in a car interior; feasibility and flammability for Engineering and Lab, not checked.

Format and visual direction: Single image or short Reel. Show the lamp closed and open side by side on a dark ground, light from inside. Palette: white gauze, pale gray base, black. You could fold your own pleated organza or polyester sheer around a small LED and film the pull.

Alt text: A hand lifts the hook of a tall, white pleated gauze lamp shade that glows above a small pale gray base on a black background.

Opening line: What if the dimmer on a lamp were just how far you pull the fabric?

Idea: Howdunit makes the textile the control. Fewer folds means a tight, bright column, more folds means a loose, soft glow. Try it with your own pleat studies and note how the fold depth changes the light, as you would with density in a woven.

Source line: https://www.dezeen.com/2026/11/05/designart-tokyo-2026-highlights/ (Dezeen, 5 Nov 2026). Event page: https://www.designboom.com/events/designart-tokyo-2026/ (designboom, dates 30 Oct to 8 Nov 2026).
