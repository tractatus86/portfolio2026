Howdunit, "Peco Poco": a lamp whose gauze shade unfolds like an accordion (product design)

"A lamp you open by pulling a thread-thin hook."

T10 Restoration, quiet and sensory care · Textile structure

Why it matters

At Designart Tokyo 2026 (30 Oct to 8 Nov, 10th edition), Tokyo studio Howdunit (Haruto Kamijo and Sho Tasaki) showed Peco Poco, a table lamp with a shade of delicate layers of gauzy textile. A small hook at the top lets you unfold it like an accordion and set it at different heights (Dezeen, 5 Nov 2026). The name is a Japanese onomatopoeia for a growling stomach, per Dezeen. The shade is a pleated, sheer structure, so height, fold depth and layer count decide how much light passes. That is a textile-structure question inside a product.

The idea

What if the dimmer on a lamp were just how far you pull the fabric?

Howdunit makes the textile the control. Fewer folds means a tight, bright column, more folds means a loose, soft glow. Try it with your own pleat studies and note how the fold depth changes the light, as you would with density in a woven.
