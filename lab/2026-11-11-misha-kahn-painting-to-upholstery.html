Misha Kahn: a painting translated into upholstery wool (product / object design)

"From brushstroke to sofa." Kahn painted a composition, had textile studio JG SWITZER weave it in wool, then rebuilt the painted arrangement across upholstered forms.

Theme: T3 Dematerialization vs rematerialization · Topic: Material innovation · Status: idea

Proposal

Theme reason: a painted image rematerialized as woven wool and upholstery.

One-line idea: "From brushstroke to sofa." Kahn painted a composition, had textile studio JG SWITZER weave it in wool, then rebuilt the painted arrangement across upholstered forms.

Why it matters: Fact: in "All Intensive Purposes" at Friedman Benda, New York (opened 29 Oct 2026, to 12 Dec 2026 per designboom radar), the new upholstered pieces began as a painted composition translated into wool by JG SWITZER; Kahn says conventional upholstery limits control over the surface (designboom, 3 Nov 2026). Interpretation: a clean example of the painting-to-weave-to-form pipeline, directly relevant to custom surface design for seating.

Format and visual direction: Single image, split in three bands: your own painted or digital color study, a woven/jacquard-style translation of it (your own render), then the upholstered form as a flat mockup. Keep color blocks bold, edges soft.

Alt text: Three stacked panels show a painted color composition, its woven textile translation, and the same pattern wrapped on a rounded sofa form.

Opening line: Paint it first, then make the fabric match the painting.

Idea: Kahn reverses the usual order: the artwork comes first and the textile is built to serve it. That is the part worth borrowing, because most upholstery starts from an existing cloth. A weave has its own resolution, so check what your painting loses and gains once it becomes yarn.

Source line: https://www.designboom.com/design/misha-kahn-upholstery-painting-marble-fabric-friedman-benda-all-intensive-purposes-interview/ (designboom, 3 Nov 2026) and https://www.designboom.com/art/exhibition-radar-november-2026/ (designboom, 1 Nov 2026, for dates)
