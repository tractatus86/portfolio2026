Open-weave gauze, then and now: Andean textiles and eight women artists (fine art / art history)

"A gauze from about the year 1000 sits beside a gauze made in 2022."

Theme: T3 Dematerialization vs rematerialization · Topic: Textile structure · Status: idea

Proposal

Theme reason: the show treats cloth as a shared knowledge system rather than one author's object, and contemporary works re-make that knowledge in new materials.

One-line idea: "A gauze from about the year 1000 sits beside a gauze made in 2022."

Why it matters: Fact: "Los hilos conductores. Textiles andinos y artistas contemporáneas" opened 12 Nov 2026 at Fundación Juan March, Palma (to 6 Feb 2027). It pairs pieces from the Paul Hughes Fine Arts collection, made between the 1st century BCE and the 10th century CE, with works by Marlene Hoffmann, Taller de Olga de Amaral, Kenia Almaraz Murillo, Ximena Garrido Lecca, Cristina Flores Pescorán, Julieth Morales, Livia Melzi and Constanza Piña. The museum describes textiles as cultural repositories and structures of memory; the page lists a Chancay gauze with 14 figures (c. 1000) and Flores Pescorán's 2022 work in mixed media and Chancay-type open-weave gauze (museum page, 12 Nov 2026). Press coverage (Diario de Mallorca, 12 Nov 2026) quotes the curator, Renata Ribeiro dos Santos, on moving away from individual authorship. Lineage: Andean cloth is often read as a record and governance tool before it is read as decoration. Interpretation: for a textile designer the open-weave gauze is a construction lesson: the figure is made by where the net is dense and where it is open, so structure is the image.

Format and visual direction: Carousel, 4 slides. (1) The c. 1000 Chancay gauze, full bleed. (2) The 2022 Flores Pescorán work beside it. (3) A line drawing of one netted figure as a grid of open and closed cells. (4) One line on density as drawing. Palette: pale thread on near-black, nothing else. Owner could shoot: a backlit sheer or leno swatch on a dark board, or draw the cell map as a vector.

Alt text: Pale netted thread forms small stepped figures against a dark ground in an old open-weave textile.

Opening line: The figure is not drawn on this cloth, it is the holes.

Idea: Put the two gauzes side by side and ask what each maker decided to leave open. I read the pairing as one structural idea carried across a thousand years: density draws, openness lets the dark through. Worth a close look at the grid logic if you design sheers or perforated trims.

Source line: https://www.march.es/es/palma/exposiciones/hilos-conductores-textiles-andinos-artistas-contemporaneas (Fundación Juan March, 12 Nov 2026); https://www.diariodemallorca.es/cultura/2026/11/12/fundacion-march-palma-une-tejidos-136374514.html (12 Nov 2026)
