Tia Ansell, "Duality": print on organza over a woven grid (fine art / fiber art)

"A city's shadows, printed on organza and laid over a woven grid."

Theme: T9 Wear, fragility and the trace of time · Topic: Textile structure · Status: idea

Proposal

Theme reason: shadows and reflections of a city are held as faint traces in a sheer layer, so the cloth records time and use.

One-line idea: "A city's shadows, printed on organza and laid over a woven grid."

Why it matters: Fact: LON Gallery presents Tia Ansell's fourth solo show, Duality. Ansell prints fragmented forms, shadows and reflections of the built environment onto Japanese organza and layers them over her hand-woven, gridded surfaces. The listed works are sublimation-dye print on Japanese organza with cotton and silk weaving, in aluminum frames (LON Gallery exhibition page, listed under 2026-08-08; show dates to confirm). Lineage: the grid in weaving as a plan of a city, repeated as rhythm and labor. Interpretation: organza over a woven ground is a construction decision, not a finish. The woven layer sets the rhythm and the sheer layer sets how much image can survive in it, which is the density and transparency question we deal with in interiors and soft materials. Hypothesis: a printed sheer over a jacquard or dobby ground could read the same way in a door panel or a pillar trim; feasibility is for Engineering.

Format and visual direction: Carousel, 3 slides. (1) The Ansell work, cropped to the grid, (2) a detail of your own organza over a woven swatch, light behind it, (3) one line on which layer carries the image. Palette: gray-green, pale blue, warm white, one dark shadow tone. Matte weave against the sheer. You could shoot your own organza over a plaid swatch on a window, or render the layering in 2D.

Alt text: A framed woven panel with a pale gray-green grid and a faint printed image of tree shadows and a dark rectangular shape, repeated in rows.

Opening line: What does a woven grid remember when you lay a shadow over it?

Idea: Ansell does not weave the picture, she weaves the ground and lets a sheer layer carry the image. For a textile designer that is a construction study: the weave decides rhythm, the organza decides how much of the city is left. Put a print on organza over one of your own constructions and see which layer wins.

Source line: https://www.longallery.com/exhibitions/tia-ansell-duality-2026-08-08 (LON Gallery, exhibition page dated 2026-08-08 in its URL; page read 2026-11-15; show end date to confirm).
