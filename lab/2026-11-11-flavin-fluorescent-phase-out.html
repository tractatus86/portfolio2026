Flavin and the fluorescent phase-out

Theme: T9 Wear, fragility and the trace of time · Topic: Material innovation · Status: idea

Proposal

  • Image-based: the faded green silk with one hole, 2026-11-06 Met 463253.jpg (Met Open Access, CC0 per references/source-records.md; https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/463253). Read from the image: olive-moss greens (about #8A9A3E on a tan ground, about #B5AA6A; eyeballed, not measured), worn pattern of stars and rosettes, one torn hole showing white backing. Its use: reference (palette and the idea of faded, holed green). It is already named as a T9 asset in the theme note.
  • Topic-based: Guggenheim Bilbao opens "Dan Flavin: Some Light" on 11 Nov 2026 (today). Its highlight is "greens crossing greens (to Piet Mondrian who lacked green)" (1966). The news that pairs with it is the global fluorescent phase-out (see below).
  • Link between them: both are green things whose material is on its way out. One is a dye and fiber that faded. The other is a lamp type whose manufacture the treaty ends in 2027.

News topics considered (pairing options)

  1. Fluorescent lamp phase-out, Minamata Convention (chosen). Fact: COP-5 (Nov 2023) set phase-out of manufacture, import and export of linear fluorescent lamps for general lighting for 2027 (triband) and 2026 (halophosphate); COP-6 (Geneva, 3 to 7 Nov 2025) restated the table. Existing lamps and stock may still be used and sold. Best fit: it touches Flavin's single material directly and gives T9 a living example.
  2. Conservation of light-based art when the lamps go. Fact: Dan Flavin Studio's conservator Steve Morse has said the work is "the light, not the light fixture" and that a replacement lamp is acceptable if it looks and performs the same (The Art Newspaper, 1 Mar 2010; https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2010/03/01/shining-a-light-on-environmental-directives). Used as supporting context, not as news (it is 2010).
  3. Museums moving from fluorescent to LED. Canadian Conservation Institute Technical Bulletin 36 on LED quality and fading (https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/conservation-preservation-publications/technical-bulletins/led-lighting-museums.html; page date to confirm). Held back: general, no Flavin hook. Not chosen: Hella Jongerius at MK&G (held back in the 2026-11-10 ideas note for a later day).

1-1 Alt text

Two overlapping translucent green bands on a dark background, with a worn green fabric swatch that has a single hole.

2-1 Opening line (hook)

The greens are made of light, and the light is going out of production.

2-2 Idea

Flavin made color from fluorescent tubes, an everyday industrial material he used as the whole medium. His 1966 "greens crossing greens" is a study in green on green. This week his show opens in Bilbao, and the treaty that ends the manufacture of fluorescent lamps for general lighting (2026 to 2027) is already law. Next to it, a 14th-century silk has faded to moss and lost a piece of itself. One color carries its age in the fiber; the other will carry it in a supply chain. Interpretation: a color that depends on one material is only as permanent as that material, which is the CMF question of what we match when a lamp, dye or yarn is discontinued. Hypothesis, not tested: the green could be rebuilt with another source (LED, dye), but whether it would read the same is to confirm. Experimental note, not a claim about any single work.

Fact, interpretation, hypothesis

  • Fact: Bilbao show dates 11 Nov 2026 to 4 Apr 2027, curated by Lauren Hinkson, more than twenty works (press release). Minamata phase-out dates per the COP-6 table: linear fluorescent triband 2027, halophosphate more than 40 W 2026. Existing stock and use remain allowed (Asahi Shimbun, https://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/15048999, article date to confirm).
  • Interpretation: the faded silk and the fluorescent tube are both "green things losing their material base". The theme link to T9 is mine, not from the theme log.
  • Hypothesis: a swatch pair under two light sources would show the point (from the 2026-11-10 idea).
