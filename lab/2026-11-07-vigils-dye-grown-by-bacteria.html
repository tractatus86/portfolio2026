Vigils: dye grown by bacteria (material innovation / colour)

"Color that grows" – a color-and-process study of bacteria-grown indigo and melanin on 3D-knitted silk.

Theme: T3 Dematerialization vs rematerialization · Topic: Material innovation · Status: idea

Proposal

Theme reason: a physical color grown by a living culture, rematerialization of dye.

One-line idea: "Color that grows" – a color-and-process study of bacteria-grown indigo and melanin on 3D-knitted silk.

Why it matters: Lineage: indigo is among the oldest dye stories in textiles; here it is produced by a living culture. Link to CMF: a way to think about faded indigo-pink palettes and unrepeatable variation on knit surfaces.

Format and visual direction: Carousel (3). Slide 1: your own swatch grid of faded indigo to pink gradients. Slide 2: process diagram, spray, 24 h growth, wash, dry. Slide 3: question slide about repeatability. Colors: faded indigo, dusty pink, undyed silk.

Alt text: Row of knitted swatches fading from pale indigo to dusty pink with a simple three-step process diagram beneath.

Opening line: A fabric dye that is alive for 24 hours, then washed away, pigment stays.

Idea: Dezeen (2 Nov 2026) reports Neri Oxman's studio sprays an E. coli (BL21) solution on tussah silk knit; the culture converts amino acids into indigo and melanin, and washing removes the bacteria. It is a prototype, with no extensive life cycle assessment; the studio says it is not an optimized industrial process. For CMF: variation is the feature, but repeat color matching is the open question.

Source line: https://www.dezeen.com/2026/11/02/neri-oxman-vigils-fabric-silk-pigment/
