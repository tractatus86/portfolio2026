Surface, gloss and wear: Alter at Designart Tokyo 2026 (product and object design)

"Show the making." A young-designer show in Tokyo where texture, dye and gloss are the content.

T9 Wear, fragility and the trace of time · Material innovation

Why it matters

Alter, second edition, ran at Nihonbashi Mitsu Hall during Designart Tokyo (30 Oct to 8 Nov 2026), with work from 75 creators, mostly in their 20s or 30s. Curator Tim Teven's group show At First Contact selected pieces drawing attention to "reflection, gloss and wear", linking them to Japanese craft where the effects of making stay visible. Other works include Capillary Drift (dyed washi stools and vessels by Yuma Kano, Gen Taniguchi of Nao Tesuki Washi, and Kurara Omichi of Uchida Dyeing Factory) and a ribbed polyurethane-rubber lamp by LS Gomma (Dezeen, 4 Nov 2026). For textile and CMF, the show is a lesson in surface as the first thing touched; dye on paper fiber behaves like dye on yarn. This is a visualization-level reading, not a feasibility claim. Show has ended (8 Nov); post it as a report, not an invitation.

The idea

Same shape, three finishes: which one shows wear first?

Teven picked work where you can still read how it was made. I would test that on my own bench: take one base color, make it matte, satin and gloss, and see which finish tells the truth about handling. Then compare it with washi dyed by capillary action, where the color moves on its own.
