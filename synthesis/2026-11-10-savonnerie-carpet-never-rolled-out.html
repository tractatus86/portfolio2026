Savonnerie: the carpet that never got rolled out (lineage)

"92 carpets, one corridor": Louis XIV's Savonnerie commission as the largest woven project of its time.

T1 Fabric as sculpture, sculpture as cloth · Textile structure

Why it matters

Lineage: commissioned 1668, woven over about 20 years after designs by Charles Le Brun, never laid as planned. Link: scale, pile depth and modular repeat are the same questions as modular carpet tile and large-format soft flooring today.

The idea

"The largest carpet ever woven was 92 pieces, and the king never used it."

The Met's "A King's Carpet: Louis XIV and the Savonnerie" is on view from 8 Sep 2026 to 5 Mar 2028 (lineage item, not new this week). Per the Met, the scheme was meant to cover the Louvre's Grande Galerie, 440 m by their figure; the show has four complete carpets, fragments, a trial weave, and an in-gallery conservation studio. The museum also describes a young, expendable workforce of trainee weavers. the interesting design question is modularity, since a single image had to survive 92 hand-woven seams.
