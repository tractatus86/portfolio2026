Stitch by stitch: Morii Darshan, "Renaissance of Hand Embroidery" at Designart Tokyo 2026 (textile art / craft)

A white house in cross-stitch and a lampshade in beads: embroidery as picture, and embroidery as surface.

T9 Wear, fragility and the trace of time · Textile structure

Why it matters

Morii Darshan, a textile and communication design studio in Gujarat, India (founded by Brinda Dudhat and Kabir, per Designart), showed "Renaissance of Hand Embroidery" at GREEN BOX IKEJIRI during Designart Tokyo 2026 (30 Oct to 8 Nov 2026). The studio works with about 180 women artisans in rural communities; the show covered cross-stitch pieces, large wall works, partitions, quilts and fine cotton appliqué curtains (designart.jp text; Lula Japan, 6 Nov 2026, reports the same). The page captions mention cross-stitch by women of the Jatt tribe and bead embroidery on a lampshade. For a designer interested in embroidery on sheer cloth, this is the opposite pole: dense, opaque, counted stitch, where density and color rhythm replace transparency. Pair it with the organza work already collected from Designart for a density-versus-sheer comparison.

The idea

How many stitches does it take to draw a house?

One piece uses thread as pixels, the other uses beads as a gradient, and both turn hours into surface. I would read them as a density study: counted stitch holds the whole picture, where your organza work lets the picture float. Put the two side by side and ask what each one hides and shows.
