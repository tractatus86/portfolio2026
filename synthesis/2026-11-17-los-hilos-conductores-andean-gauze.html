Open-weave gauze, then and now: Andean textiles and eight women artists (fine art / art history)

"A gauze from about the year 1000 sits beside a gauze made in 2022."

T3 Dematerialization vs rematerialization · Textile structure

Why it matters

"Los hilos conductores. Textiles andinos y artistas contemporáneas" opened 12 Nov 2026 at Fundación Juan March, Palma (to 6 Feb 2027). It pairs pieces from the Paul Hughes Fine Arts collection, made between the 1st century BCE and the 10th century CE, with works by Marlene Hoffmann, Taller de Olga de Amaral, Kenia Almaraz Murillo, Ximena Garrido Lecca, Cristina Flores Pescorán, Julieth Morales, Livia Melzi and Constanza Piña. The museum describes textiles as cultural repositories and structures of memory; the page lists a Chancay gauze with 14 figures (c. 1000) and Flores Pescorán's 2022 work in mixed media and Chancay-type open-weave gauze (museum page, 12 Nov 2026). Press coverage (Diario de Mallorca, 12 Nov 2026) quotes the curator, Renata Ribeiro dos Santos, on moving away from individual authorship. Lineage: Andean cloth is often read as a record and governance tool before it is read as decoration. For a textile designer the open-weave gauze is a construction lesson: the figure is made by where the net is dense and where it is open, so structure is the image.

The idea

The figure is not drawn on this cloth, it is the holes.

Put the two gauzes side by side and ask what each maker decided to leave open. I read the pairing as one structural idea carried across a thousand years: density draws, openness lets the dark through. Worth a close look at the grid logic if you design sheers or perforated trims.
