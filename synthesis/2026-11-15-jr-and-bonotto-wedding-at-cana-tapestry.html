JR and Giovanni Bonotto: Veronese's "Wedding at Cana" woven as a tapestry (fine art / art history)

"A 1563 painting, turned into a photo, turned into cloth."

T3 Dematerialization vs rematerialization · Material innovation

Why it matters

At the 61st Venice Biennale, JR's project Il Gesto reinterprets Paolo Veronese's 1563 painting The Wedding at Cana as a tapestry woven with Fondazione Bonotto, 4.30 m by 7.80 m, on view until 24 Nov 2026. It took 600 hours of research and weaving. Blue yarns and black shadow tones come from recycled plastic from household chemical and soap containers, combined with virgin wool, organic cotton and Japanese washi paper. Bonotto says the file was too large for the mechanical looms, which kept crashing (designboom, 9 May 2026). Lineage: Veronese's painting hung at San Giorgio Maggiore for over two centuries before moving to the Louvre (same page); tapestry as the old medium for carrying painting into rooms. The story is about construction limits: yarn count, loom file size and yarn choice decide how much of a photograph survives in cloth.

The idea

A painting from 1563 is now a tapestry that crashed the looms.

JR starts from a photograph and Bonotto has to decide, yarn by yarn, what the loom can hold. For a textile designer the interesting part is the translation: recycled-plastic blues, washi and wool each give a different surface. Pick one image of your own and ask how many yarns it really needs.
