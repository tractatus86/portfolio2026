Tia Ansell, "Duality": print on organza over a woven grid (fine art / fiber art)

"A city's shadows, printed on organza and laid over a woven grid."

T9 Wear, fragility and the trace of time · Textile structure

Why it matters

LON Gallery presents Tia Ansell's fourth solo show, Duality. Ansell prints fragmented forms, shadows and reflections of the built environment onto Japanese organza and layers them over her hand-woven, gridded surfaces. The listed works are sublimation-dye print on Japanese organza with cotton and silk weaving, in aluminum frames. Lineage: the grid in weaving as a plan of a city, repeated as rhythm and labor. Organza over a woven ground is a construction decision, not a finish. A printed sheer over a jacquard or dobby ground could read the same way in a door panel or a pillar trim; feasibility is for Engineering.

The idea

What does a woven grid remember when you lay a shadow over it?

Ansell does not weave the picture, she weaves the ground and lets a sheer layer carry the image. For a textile designer that is a construction study: the weave decides rhythm, the organza decides how much of the city is left. Put a print on organza over one of your own constructions and see which layer wins.
