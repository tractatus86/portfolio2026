Poured, not woven: Carolina Zimbalist's biopolymer over silk (apparel / material innovation)

"A coating that becomes the cloth." A hand-poured, petroleum-free biopolymer dries into a crinkled gloss on red silk.

T9 Wear, fragility and the trace of time · Material innovation

Why it matters

Artist Carolina Zimbalist made a one-off dress for Emma Watson, worn at the LACMA Art+Film Gala on 7 Nov 2026. The material uses vegetable glycerin and proteins such as pea powder, is poured and dried over silk, and favors byproducts (Dezeen, 18 Nov 2026). She now prefers "biopolymer" to "bioplastic" and calls the piece an exploration of impermanence (Dezeen; WWD). It is a one-off, not a tested or scaled textile. It moves a surface finish into construction. The pour decides drape, crinkle and gloss, the opposite of cut-and-sew, and raises CMF questions of hand, durability and end of life.

The idea

What if the finish was the fabric?

Zimbalist pours a material onto silk and lets it set, so surface and structure arrive together. Read it as a construction question for a soft-materials designer: where does the coating end and the cloth begin, and what does the skin do when it wears?
