Fatim Soumaré, "Djilor": a spool of thread as a map of place

Seven monumental cotton works whose threads run like vines and carry objects gathered from Cape Verde to the Maghreb and Brazil.

T6 Place and nature · Textile structure

Why it matters

Selebe Yoon, Dakar, shows "Djilor", Fatim Soumaré's first solo show at the gallery, 6 Nov 2026 to 27 Mar 2027, opening 18 Nov 2026, alongside the 16th Dakar Biennale. Seven new works combine cotton with terracotta beads, shells, spinning wheels, anchors, old manillas, sand, fish scales and wool offcuts from Amazigh carpets (gallery page). Soumaré learned textile craft from her mother, a traditional dyer. Structure first: the thread is the plan, and knots and loops decide where weight and objects sit. For an interiors and soft-materials designer it is a lesson in open cotton constructions that hold mass.

The idea

What if the first line on a map was a thread?

Soumaré starts from one spool and builds a landscape. Compare open net with dense tuft in the same cotton: that contrast is the construction lesson. Set her hanging beside your own open-structure sample and ask how few yarns still carry a place.
