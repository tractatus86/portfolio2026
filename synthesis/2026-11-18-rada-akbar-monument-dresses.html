A dress as a monument: Rada Akbar at TAMAT (fine art / textile art)

"A dress you visit instead of wear." Akbar's monument-dresses, each named for a specific Afghan woman, shown in a museum of tapestry and textile arts.

T9 Wear, fragility and the trace of time · Apparel

Why it matters

TAMAT in Tournai (museum of tapestry and textile arts) presents Akbar's first solo show in Belgium, 7 Nov 2026 to 14 Mar 2027. Akbar is an Afghan artist living in exile in France; the museum describes her monument-dresses as tributes to Afghan women (queens, poets, artists) and frames the show around a "matrimoine", a heritage of forms and gestures passed down through women, with embroidery as a central thread and a new textile work shown only at TAMAT (TAMAT page; ArtWorldBrief; Septmille). Septmille reports that she works with Afghan artisans skilled in embroidery, ceramic-tile motifs and traditional jewelry. Lineage: Tournai was a major tapestry center in the 15th and 16th centuries (per Septmille). For a soft-materials designer, the dress works as structure, surface and archive at once; the embellishment carries the content, not decoration.

The idea

What if the dress were the memorial?

Akbar names each garment for one woman, so the textile stops being a surface and becomes a portrait. As someone who designs surfaces for use, I read it as a reminder that embellishment can carry a record: who made it, who it is for, what it refuses to forget. Put a stitched sheer next to a printed one and ask which one remembers.
