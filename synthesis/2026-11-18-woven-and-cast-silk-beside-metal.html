Woven and cast: Ming and Qing silks beside Japanese metalwork (art history / object design)

"Silk that glows like metal, metal that shimmers like silk."

T8 Asia and postcolonial frame · Textile structure

Why it matters

The Smithsonian's National Museum of Asian Art opened "Woven and Cast: Chinese Textiles and Japanese Metalwork from the Shirley Z. Johnson Collection" on 7 Nov 2026 (runs to 28 Nov 2027). It sets late-imperial (Ming and Qing, 15th to 19th century) Chinese textiles beside 20th and 21st-century Japanese metalwork to show shared texture and luminosity. Embroidery silks, some wrapped with peacock-feather filaments or metal, sit next to cast and hammered gold, silver, copper and alloys; a two-part video shows textile conservators and two Japanese metal artists, Ōsumi Yukie and Tanaka Terukazu (museum release, 30 Sep 2026). For a CMF designer, it is a clean case of luster as structure, thread wrapping and metal surface solving the same problem of light. For automotive and interiors, it reads as a reference for shimmer and depth, not a technique claim. Note: the page was opened through a text extraction; I did not see the gallery.

The idea

Two materials, five centuries apart, doing the same thing with light.

The show looks for what a thread and a cast alloy share: how they hold and throw light. I would put one metallic yarn next to one metal sample and photograph both at the same angle, then ask what changes when the surface moves with the body.
