Let the fungus finish the cloth: "Ways of Growing / Modos de crescer" in Lisbon (material design / bio-design)

Twenty artists and designers treat plants, fungi and bacteria as co-makers, from kombucha-cellulose cyanotypes to chestnut-dyed linen.

T9 Wear, fragility and the trace of time · Sustainability

Why it matters

Designboom (16 Nov 2026) reports "Ways of Growing / Modos de crescer" at the Gallery of the Faculty of Fine Arts, University of Lisbon (FBAUL), 22 Oct to 28 Nov 2026, the end of the two-year Free Radicals project led by Haenke. Works include cyanotypes printed on kombucha-derived bacterial cellulose and toned with plant extracts; Alejandra Díaz-Guerra Acedo's bio-based textile experiment pairing traditional Portuguese crochet with living mycelium, letting fungi decompose cotton buried in contrasting soils; and Eleonora Edreva's textile poem woven from vintage linen and chestnut dyes. A book, "Free Radicals: Attuning to Plants in Creative Practices" (Onomatopee, eds. Sasha Střelcová and Linsey Rendell), launches with it. For soft-materials work the useful point is process, not product: the maker sets conditions (soil, time, organism) and the finish arrives later. That is a different relation to durability than most CMF briefs, and a way to talk about wear and patina as designed outcomes. Feasibility and durability for any real application are unknown (Engineering and Lab to judge); this is a visualization of an idea.

The idea

What if the last step of finishing a fabric was handing it to a fungus?

Instead of a clean sample, show the stage when the material is still changing. Pair the translucent cellulose close-up with the installation view of leaf litter and cloth: the first is surface, the second is the condition that makes it.
