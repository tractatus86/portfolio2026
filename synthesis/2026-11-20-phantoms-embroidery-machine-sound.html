The sound of an embroidery machine, stitched into organza: SELMA, "PHANTOMS" at alter., Designart Tokyo 2026 (textile art / craft)

What does an old embroidery machine look like if you stitch its sound?

T7 Sound, performance, collaboration · Textile structure

Why it matters

PHANTOMS, shown at alter. during Designart Tokyo 2026 (opening hours listed 29 Oct to 1 Nov 2026), is an audiovisual textile project by the Basel-based designer and architect Selma with Walter Sonderegger, a fourth-generation Swiss embroidery artisan, and Yang Yi, a textile artisan of Kam (Dong) heritage in Guizhou, China. Recordings of the world's first embroidery machines, more than a century old, are translated into digital patterns; three semi-transparent textiles float in space; two new works for 2026 are hand-embroidered and hand-dyed with organic indigo, completing a triptych (alter. project page). Yang Yi contributed a hand-dyed textile sample that Selma then embroidered (alter. creator page). Institutional partners include the Textile Museum St. Gallen and the Saurer Museum Arbon. Embroidery here is treated as hidden structure, a stitch pattern that is also a score.

The idea

An embroidery machine made this sound a century ago; now the sound is the pattern.

Selma converts the recorded rhythm of historic machines into stitch figures and has two artisans carry them out in indigo and thread. The image works because the pattern comes from a measured source, not from decoration. Pair a waveform with its stitched figure and the translation reads in one glance.
