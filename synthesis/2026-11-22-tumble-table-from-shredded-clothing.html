Clothing pressed into a tabletop: Union Design's "Tumble" for KFI Studios (product / material design)

A side table whose top is made of shredded, compressed clothing, with the variation left in on purpose.

T9 Wear, fragility and the trace of time · Material innovation

Why it matters

Architizer named Tumble one of its 2026 A+Product of the Year winners (10 Nov 2026). Per the article, Union Design designed it for KFI Studios: a round top of shredded and compressed recycled textiles on a solid wood base with a circular cutout taken from the tumble-dry laundry symbol; no two tops are the same and the variation is left visible. The article calls it strong enough for commercial use. It moves textile waste from fiber to board, a stone-like surface where the fiber identity survives as speckle. For a CMF designer the question is the finish: how much of the source fabric should still read? Test data, binder and fiber mix are not stated in the text I saw. The same logic could suit trim or door-panel inserts, but that is untested and for Engineering to judge.

The idea

A tabletop made from clothes, and you can still see them in it.

Tumble treats waste as a finish rather than hiding it, so the speckle is the design. I read it as a color-and-grain decision: light top, dark top, same method. Compare it with how a CMF board handles recycled content, and ask where the flecks should stop being charming and start being noise.
