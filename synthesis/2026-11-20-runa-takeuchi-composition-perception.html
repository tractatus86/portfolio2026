Same cloth, different look: Runa Takeuchi, "Composition / Perception" at Designart Tokyo 2026 (textile design)

One sheet of fabric, five ways to make it look different from every angle.

T9 Wear, fragility and the trace of time · Material innovation

Why it matters

In the UNDER30 section of Designart Tokyo 2026 (30 Oct to 3 Nov 2026, KK Line, Tokyo), Runa Takeuchi shows a textile collection about how fabric changes with viewpoint, distance, light and time (her page). Techniques named on the page: interference pearl pigment screen-printed on chambray ("Oboro"); thick-layer hand screen printing by Kyoto craftspeople ("Waku"); a shrinkable film bonded to chambray organza and peeled off after heat shrinking to leave irregular relief ("Une"); cut pieces joined through slits without adhesive ("Mupi"); thin color film applied to both sides of hand-pleated organza and torn away ("Kakera"). The collection is on sale by the meter (from 1 m) for three of the textiles. Coverage: DesignWanted and SCALE mention the collection (both opened via search text).

The idea

A textile has no single color; it has a color from where you stand.

Takeuchi makes the change of appearance the subject, and builds it into the cloth with print, shrinkage, tearing and slits. For a designer who works on construction, the instructive part is how little she adds: a film, a pigment, a cut. Show one swatch at three angles next to her pieces and ask which change is the print and which is the structure.
