A body as a lantern: Sian Fan, "The Body is a Relic (how the light gets in)" at V&A East Storehouse (fine art / textile art)

Three life-size hanging figures, lit from inside, carry woven and embroidered images of a family's places, so the cloth is the archive.

T9 Wear, fragility and the trace of time · Textile structure

Why it matters

The V&A lists "The Body is a Relic (how the light gets in)" by Sian Fan at V&A East Storehouse, Gallery 3, free, until 20 June 2027. Per the V&A blog interview (18 Nov 2026), the work is three suspended, body-like sculptures on rounded bamboo frames, covered in fabrics of differing opacity, with a light bar strung inside each so they read as lanterns; the textiles carry images from her research trip rendered through embroidery and weaving (Hakka architecture cut-outs, a patterned window glass, koi, lion dancers). She names the Hakka "Liangmao" hat and Hakka scrap-cloth practice in the V&A collection as starting points. It is part of the Design Trust Fellowship at V&A East, theme "The Stories of Clothes". For a textile designer this is a construction study in opacity: the same image reads differently through layers of sheer, woven and embroidered cloth when light passes through, which is close to the organza and embroidery interest. It also sits in the lineage of the tapestry as a portable archive.

The idea

What if the only heirloom you have is your own body, and the cloth that covers it?

The pairing is the image through the cloth: a lit core behind layers of different opacity, so the weave and embroidery decide what you see. Designer to designer: this is opacity as a design tool, and the question underneath it is what a textile can hold when nothing else was kept.
