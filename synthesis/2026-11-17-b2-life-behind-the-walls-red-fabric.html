Silhouettes on sheer red cloth: B2 Architecture, "Life Behind the Walls" (architecture / exhibition design)

"Translucent red walls that show people only as shadows."

T4 Institutional critique and the apparatus · Textile structure

Why it matters

B2 Architecture (Prague) built a 1,500 m2 exhibition on life in North Korea, based on a children's book by Nina Špitálníková, from red-painted timber frames hung with red and pink translucent fabric; a darker second gallery uses tall translucent panels printed with human silhouettes that overlap portraits and testimonies of defectors. The structure is lightweight modular timber and stretched textile; the timber went to another project and the textiles and flooring to several theatres (Dezeen, 16 Nov 2026). Sheer printed cloth works here as a spatial layer: it lets you glimpse, not read, which is what the exhibition is about. For an interiors and soft-materials designer it is a clear case of fabric doing architectural work, with reuse planned from the start. Subject is sensitive; keep the post about the material and spatial method, not the politics.

The idea

A sheer wall can hide people and show them at the same time.

The design choice to admire is the fabric: a printed sheer lets silhouettes read from far away and dissolve up close. Think of it as a density study at room scale. The reuse of the textiles by theatres is a good, concrete note on end of life for event fabrics.
