Tapestry that leaves the wall: Maria Assumpció Raventós, "Filant la natura" at Fundació Felícia Fuster (fine art / art history)

A flat weave with knotted pile rising out of it, so one cloth carries two constructions.

T1 Fabric as sculpture, sculpture as cloth · Textile structure

Why it matters

The Fundació Felícia Fuster in Barcelona shows nine tapestries by Maria Assumpció Raventós (born 1930, Sant Sadurní d'Anoia), 12 Nov 2026 to 8 Jan 2027, free entry. Per the foundation, she trained in Barcelona, worked within the Catalan tapestry school with Josep Grau-Garriga, and learned knotting techniques from the Argentine artist Susana Rolando that give the weave three-dimensional relief; the works use her own dyes on virgin fiber (foundation text, Catalan). The curator is Elina Norandi. ** Catalan tapestry of the 1960s to 70s is the moment when weaving moved off the wall and into volume. For a textile designer this is a construction lesson: flat tapestry weave beside long knotted pile in one piece, so density, loft and shadow carry the picture. It also speaks to interiors, where pile height is a surface and acoustic decision. A small sample pairing plain weave with a cut-pile strip could test the same effect at swatch scale. Opening was 12 Nov, so this is slightly past the 48-hour window (widened, said plainly).

The idea

Same cloth, two constructions: one flat, one rising off it.

Raventós lets the knot do what the weft cannot, so the tapestry stops being a picture and starts being a surface with depth. I read it as a pile-height study: where does flat end and relief begin, and how much does the shadow do? Put it next to your own sample and compare how each one holds light.
