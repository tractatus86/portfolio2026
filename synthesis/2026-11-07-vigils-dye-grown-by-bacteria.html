Vigils: dye grown by bacteria (material innovation / colour)

"Color that grows" – a color-and-process study of bacteria-grown indigo and melanin on 3D-knitted silk.

T3 Dematerialization vs rematerialization · Material innovation

Why it matters

Lineage: indigo is among the oldest dye stories in textiles; here it is produced by a living culture. Link to CMF: a way to think about faded indigo-pink palettes and unrepeatable variation on knit surfaces.

The idea

A fabric dye that is alive for 24 hours, then washed away, pigment stays.

Dezeen (2 Nov 2026) reports Neri Oxman's studio sprays an E. coli (BL21) solution on tussah silk knit; the culture converts amino acids into indigo and melanin, and washing removes the bacteria. It is a prototype, with no extensive life cycle assessment; the studio says it is not an optimized industrial process. For CMF: variation is the feature, but repeat color matching is the open question.
