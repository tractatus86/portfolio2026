A hundred years of tsuzure: Paola Lenti and Kawashima Selkon, "Woven Landscapes" at Designart Tokyo 2026 (textile / interiors)

An Italian furniture and textile brand shows a rug drawn from the earth's layers, woven by the Kyoto house that has pursued tsuzure-ori (weft-faced tapestry weave) for about a century.

T6 Place and nature · Textile structure

Why it matters

Paola Lenti Tokyo held "Woven Landscapes" during Designart Tokyo 2026, 30 Oct to 7 Nov 2026, with Kawashima Selkon Textiles (Paola Lenti Tokyo page, 13 Oct 2026, updated 31 Oct; press release 26 Oct 2026). The page describes tsuzure-ori as a technique that draws pictures through the combination of warp and weft, with up to hundreds or thousands of colored yarns; it names Kawashima's Meiji-era large work for the Chicago World's Fair, a tapestry for the Osaka-Kansai Expo, and the strata artwork shown at Paola Lenti Milano during Milan Design Week 2026. The show also presents the brand's "Chromatic Alphabet", 44 color families, and the Giunco armchair, which reinterprets a classic rattan-woven chair in the brand's own high-performance yarn. Strong for a CMF and interiors designer: it joins hand construction (tsuzure) and a structured color system (44 families) with outdoor-grade yarn, and the strata motif is a clear example of nature turned into pattern by construction rather than print. Exhibition closed on 7 Nov, so this is a retrospective look; opening is beyond 48 h (widened).

The idea

Hundreds of colored yarns, one picture of the earth's layers.

The pairing shows two ways to turn nature into cloth: the Kyoto house draws it with weft, the Italian brand sorts it into a color alphabet. Put the strata weave next to the orange sample table and the question is the same: how much of a landscape can a textile carry before it needs a print? Designer to designer: it is a clean example of structure doing the work of image.
