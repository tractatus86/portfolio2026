Cloth as a gift: "Tapa: Art of the Gift in Polynesia" at The Met (art history / textile art)

Thirteen works of 19th-century bark cloth from Fiji, Samoa, Futuna and Niue, shown as instruments of gifting and diplomacy rather than as craft specimens.

T3 Dematerialization vs rematerialization · Material innovation

Why it matters

The Met opened "Tapa: Art of the Gift in Polynesia" on 2 Nov 2026 in Gallery 359 (Michael C. Rockefeller Wing, In-Focus gallery); it runs to 12 Dec 2027. The Met's text says tapa (masi in Fiji, siapo in Samoa, hiapo in Niue) is made from the softened inner bark of trees, by collectives of women, with designs painted, stamped or stenciled; the tradition is more than 3,500 years old and wrapping the body in tapa enhances status (mana). The show includes a Niuean hiapo on loan from Te Papa and a large monochrome painting by John Puhiatau Pule (b. 1962). Curator: Maia Nuku. Lineage: ceremonial gifting and exchange, in a tradition developed across the islands of Southeast Asia. For a textile and CMF designer this is a surface built from joined strips with a graphic system on top (borders, grids, stepped bands, stripes), a useful counter-example to woven structure: pattern and construction arrive in one move, and the cloth carries social value. A grid-and-border logic like this also reads well beside printed or jacquard interior surfaces. Opening date is beyond 48 h (widened to about three weeks; the Met press release is dated 27 Oct 2026).

The idea

Before it was a pattern on a screen, it was a gift you could wrap a person in.

Set the masi kesa panel beside a modern repeat. The grid is as exact as any jacquard, yet every line is hand-stamped or painted on a surface made from beaten strips. As someone who builds surfaces from constructed cloth, I read this as a lesson in how little structure it takes to hold a lot of graphic order, and how the cloth's use (gift, diplomacy) is part of its design. Ask what a surface for an interior would look like if its job were to carry status, not just look good.
