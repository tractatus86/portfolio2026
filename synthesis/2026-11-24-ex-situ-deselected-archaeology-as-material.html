Ex Situ: deselected archaeological finds become design material (product / object design)

"What the museum would not keep, the designer can still use." Nine Dutch makers work with archaeological finds that were deselected from archives.

T9 Wear, fragility and the trace of time · Sustainability

Why it matters

Dezeen (23 Nov 2026) reports Ex Situ, initiated by designer Marisa Klaster with archaeologist René Isarin. Deselected finds (duplicates, common types, poorly preserved) are given to nine practitioners with the province of South Holland's permission. Examples named: footwear designer Judith Sanders turned a section of a Roman Limes road (dated 125 AD, under Hadrian) into the heel of a pair of high-heeled shoes and reworked medieval leather into a sneaker silhouette; Daan Simons made "Remembrance Pots" from 600-year-old ship wood. A preview showed at Dutch Design Week in October; the public show is planned in The Hague. Isarin notes that a commercial context, for example a fashion brand selling the shoes, is more contested. For a soft-materials designer the question is provenance as a design property, and what "recycled" means when the feedstock has a documented age. Relevant to leather and bio-based interiors where story and traceability sell. Not a textile project; I include it as the product-design pick, and the link to textiles is the leather and the numbered labelling of origin.

The idea

If the archive does not want it, is it waste or raw material?

Ex Situ treats age as a surface quality instead of a defect, and it keeps the provenance numbers visible. As a designer who works with wear, I read this as a CMF question: how much of a material's history do you leave on, and who gets to say what is off-limits? Ask the same of a worn leather hide from a discarded seat.
