Same fiber, same weave, different white: Shinichiro Yoshida, "The White of Consciousness" (fine art / textile collection)

"One weave, one fiber, and still no two whites alike." A Kyoto artist lines up hemp and ramie cloths of the same plain weave and lets small differences in tension and twist do the talking.

T10 Restoration, quiet and sensory care · Textile structure

Why it matters

Hosoo Gallery in Kyoto shows Shinichiro Yoshida's new installation "The White of Consciousness" from 6 Nov 2026 to 20 Mar 2027. Yoshida (born Kyoto, 1948) has spent nearly four decades collecting and researching historical Japanese bast-fiber cloth (about 1,200 specimens per the gallery). His earlier "White" (shown at Hosoo in 2021) used hemp in one plain weave; the new version adds ramie, and the gallery says tension and yarn twist show when the cloths hang side by side. He also met Joseph Beuys in West Germany in 1977 (per the gallery profile). For a textile designer this is a construction study at gallery scale: hold fiber and structure constant, and surface, density and light become the content. It is a good counterweight to loud material news. Lineage: Edo-period bast-fiber cloth as the source collection.

The idea

If fiber and weave are identical, what is left to see?

Yoshida strips the variables until only tension and twist remain, and the eye learns to read them as white against white. As someone who thinks in constructions, I find that the strongest kind of argument for cloth as a primary material: no color, no pattern, just how the yarn was spun and beaten in. Pair it with a close-up of your own plain weave and ask which small decision you can actually see.
